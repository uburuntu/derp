# Scheduler

- Owns `jobs`: a reminder (`message`: posts the text) or a scheduled prompt (`prompt`: Derp answers it), once or repeating (`daily`, `weekdays`, `weekly`, `monthly`) at a wall-clock time in the chat's time zone (`chats.timezone`, set through `change_setting`; chats without one run on UTC).
- `jobs.py`: the rules — next occurrence from `anchor_at` (the first scheduled time: its wall time and day of month survive DST gaps and short months; missed firings skipped, never replayed), `rezone` (a new chat zone keeps repeating jobs at their local wall time and day; one-offs keep their instant, and so does every job of a chat that had no zone, since those were placed as instants), `resume`, `live_jobs`, `act_on_job` (pause / resume / cancel, a `Change` — time, text, kind, repeat, in place; a new time brings a paused job or a fired one-off back on, a new repeat alone moves the job to that rule's first occurrence from the anchor (`first_after`), and a job that repeats after the change needs an admin — and a `Snooze`; `may_touch`: its creator or an admin, except that a change to what a prompt costs its creator (making one, rewording it, its repeat, turning it back into a reminder) is the creator's alone since every firing is paid from their wallet; the one rule behind the agent's tools, the /settings jobs page, the Resume and the Snooze buttons; each surface only words the outcome), limits per chat and per person (`has_room`), ISO local-time parsing, and `show_time` (a `tg://time` Markdown date that Telegram renders in each viewer's time zone).
- Snooze: a fired reminder (`message` jobs) carries `SnoozeJob` buttons (`SNOOZES`: +10 min, +1 h, tomorrow at that local time) holding the firing's `runs`, so a press after the job fired again is refused (`fired`). A one-off moves to the new time; a repeating job keeps its series and gets one extra one-off (`snooze_of` points at the series; a later snooze moves it; pausing, resuming and cancelling the series carry to it, and one whose moment passed while paused is cancelled on resume). Handled with the jobs page in `settings/panel.py`.
- Races: every action locks its row (`FOR UPDATE`) and the worker claims with `SKIP LOCKED`, moving the job on in the claim; an edit or snooze racing a firing lands wholly before the claim (the job is skipped that tick and fires on the new terms) or after it (seeing the moved-on row), never double-firing or losing one.
- `worker.py`: one worker ticks every 20 s, claims due jobs (up to the free firing slots — `Firings` runs them as background tasks so a slow prompt never delays other chats) with `SKIP LOCKED` and moves them on in the same transaction before firing, so a job fires at most once per occurrence. Prompts run through `Runtime.answer` as an id-0 invocation from the creator (`trigger='job'`, never stored), funded at fire time; an unfunded firing posts a note and `MISS_LIMIT` in a row pause the job; that note carries a Resume button (`jobs.ResumeJob`, handled with the jobs page in `settings/panel.py`). A chat refusing Derp's messages (the runtime's `Outcome.refused`) pauses its jobs; Derp removed from a chat pauses all its jobs and a member leaving pauses theirs (`pause_jobs`, hooked in `history.members`). Prompts never merge into the creator's newer messages (id 0 is exempt in `Lanes`).
- Scheduling itself is the agent's `schedule` tool (`agent/tools/scheduler.py`): anyone schedules one-offs; repeating jobs in groups need an admin. A firing prompt neither schedules nor edits jobs. A wall-clock time in a chat with no zone is refused with a nudge to ask where they are (relative times and explicit offsets need none).
- Retention: finished jobs are deleted after 30 days; `/forget` (everything) deletes the person's jobs in that chat.
